# 📋 Monitoring & Data Quality in Curriculum Development

This case study was developed while working at **SLO**, the Dutch national institute for curriculum development. I worked on monitoring the development of new curriculum objectives, translating policy requirements into measurable criteria and analysing products across multiple development teams.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- **Policy → Measurable Criteria:** Translating broad policy requirements into concrete, assessable indicators.
- **Data Quality & Traceability:** Linking every assessment to its underlying source and evidence.
- **Quality Monitoring:** Applying consistent criteria across multiple teams, development rounds and products.
- **Evidence-Based Reporting:** Combining scores with the evidence behind each assessment.
- **Process Improvement:** Identifying structural issues and improving the monitoring approach based on practical experience.

## 👤 My Role

- Broke down broad requirements from the Ministry of Education into **specific, assessable indicators**.
- Ensured each indicator could be traced back to its source document, version and relevant passage.
- Tested the indicators on real curriculum products and refined them when they proved too broad or difficult to interpret consistently.
- Worked with multiple assessors and kept differences in judgement visible rather than simply averaging them away.
- Required evidence for each assessment, linking scores to the specific passages on which they were based.
- Developed documentation and guidance to make the monitoring approach consistent and repeatable.
- Produced reports for individual curriculum areas and contributed to aggregated reporting for programme management.
- Contributed to additional survey and interview research on the curriculum development process.

## 📈 Outcomes

- A structured monitoring instrument translating broad policy requirements into **traceable and repeatable product assessments**.
- Application across **nine curriculum areas**, multiple development rounds and intermediate products.
- Reports per curriculum area, discussed directly with the relevant development teams.
- Aggregated signals for programme management, making recurring issues visible across teams.
- A documented methodology and guidance for applying the approach consistently.
- Contributed to an international comparative study of curriculum and examination systems in **six European countries**.

## 🔍 Why This Matters

A requirement can be clear at a policy level while still being difficult to assess consistently in practice.

This project taught me how to translate broad requirements into **measurable criteria, structured data and evidence-based reporting**, while keeping the connection to the original source visible.

It also strengthened my experience in working between **subject-matter experts, data and decision-makers**: defining what should be measured, checking the quality of the underlying evidence, and turning the results into information that can support decisions.
